# Analytical methodology

## Data contracts and grains
M5 is a daily product-store recorded-sales panel. Evaluation sales is preferred over validation sales; day columns are discovered rather than hard-coded. Wide input is scanned by DuckDB, unpivoted and joined to calendar on `d` and weekly price on `(item_id, store_id, wm_yr_wk)`. Missing prices on positive demand fail the pipeline. Zero sales with missing price contribute zero revenue, retaining prelaunch/zero-demand days. Calendar dates must be contiguous; there is no automatic gap imputation. Both events are retained through a bridge. Derived rolling quantities end at the prior day.

DataCo is an independent order-line panel. `order_item_id` is unique; repeated order IDs are expected. Delivery outcomes collapse to one order only after checking consistent shipment attributes. Multi-shipment order conflicts fail with an explicit error because no genuine shipment key has been established. Category delivery uses distinct order-category pairs and is not additive across categories. Profitability defaults to `order_item_total` and `benefit_per_order`; these mappings are configurable and must be checked against the downloaded data description. The loader never adds the separate order-profit alias to benefit. Missing optional metrics remain unavailable; missing identity/date columns fail. Negative sales/quantity are flagged and excluded from headline profitability, not silently reinterpreted as normal demand. Extreme profits are retained and flagged.

## Cohort and EDA
Sample stores and series are ranked by trailing 90-day revenue before the earliest backtest. This avoids using validation outcomes to choose easy/high-volume series. Full mode includes all series. Sample conclusions apply only to the selected cohort, not all Walmart products. ABC uses cumulative revenue before the current item-store pair: the item crossing 80% belongs to A and the item crossing 95% to B. Zero-total portfolios are C. XYZ thresholds 0.5 and 1.0 CV are configurable planning heuristics, not universal scientific cutoffs; zero share >=0.5 forces Z. Demand-volume thirds use training-period mean ranks. Evaluation segment labels are frozen at test origin.

EDA exports daily/weekly/monthly summaries; state, store, item, category and department breakdowns; weekday/month/event/SNAP profiles; zero frequency; volatility; concentration and ABC-XYZ; and price-demand correlations. Price/event differences are descriptive associations confounded by seasonality, assortment, stock availability and selection. SNAP reflects benefits-program calendar eligibility; M5 has no definitive promotion flag. Do not describe correlation as price elasticity or causal event lift.

## Forecast experiment
Three non-overlapping chronological windows default to 28 days each: calibration, model selection, and locked final test. If inventory replay is set to 90 days, these windows become 90 days and the production horizon stays 28. Origins are anchored to the last observed date. At least 180 pre-backtest days are required. All models are compared on the identical selected item-store cohort. Naive, 7-day seasonal naive, 28-day seasonal naive and a fixed 28-day moving mean are genuine multi-step baselines.

The global LightGBM Poisson regressor uses shifted lags, shifted rolling means/standard deviations, last-known price, calendar, events, SNAP and categorical identifiers. Training is deterministically capped at 500,000 rows from the latest 365 days per origin to bound memory. Categorical vocabularies are learned on training rows only; unknown levels map to missing. Inference is recursive: after day one, lag features use earlier predictions, never held-out actuals. Price is frozen at the last observed origin price in every backtest and production forecast. Calendar events and SNAP are treated as known in advance; validate that assumption before live use. Current observed training prices are exogenous features; holding future prices flat creates a potential covariate shift, explicitly preferable to pretending realized prices were known. Historical demand has no observed stock availability, so the target is sales rather than unconstrained demand.

Aggregate additive seasonal exponential smoothing and SARIMA(1,0,0)(1,0,0,7) are separate practical diagnostics. They forecast the portfolio total, not individual pairs, and are excluded from item-store model selection. No automatic reconciliation or allocation claims are made.

Selection uses lowest WAPE in the selection fold, with MAE/model-name tie-breakers. It is locked before inspecting test performance. The advanced model may lose. MAE/RMSE are unit errors; WAPE is sum absolute error / sum actual; bias is sum(prediction-actual)/sum actual. MAPE excludes zero actuals and is secondary. RMSSE scale uses training first differences after the first nonzero demand; constant/all-zero series have undefined scales and are excluded from RMSSE only. Pooled RMSSE is sqrt(mean(error²/series scale)); this is not mean of per-series RMSSE and not official hierarchical WRMSSE. WRMSSE is intentionally not claimed because the 12-level weighted hierarchy is not implemented.

80%/95% intervals use signed residual quantiles by forecast horizon pooled across series in earlier calibration windows. Bands include the point estimate, clip at zero, and have approximate coverage only. Held-out test coverage is measured. Pooling sacrifices series-specific heteroskedasticity; improve with scaled/segment residuals or calibrated quantiles. Production intervals use only pre-test calibration/selection residuals. Do not sum item bands into aggregate confidence intervals.

## Inventory policy and replay
Each item-store has simulated supplier, lead time, cost, review and stock assumptions. Forecast mean replaces historical demand in expected lead-time demand. Error standard deviation from pre-test model residuals is preferred over raw demand standard deviation; raw variability is a fallback. The normal approximation assumes independent demand errors and lead times:

`SS = z * sqrt(L * error_std² + mean_daily_forecast² * lead_time_std²)`.

`ROP = L * mean_daily_forecast + SS` is the continuous-review reference. The implemented periodic-review policy protects `L + review_period` days, uses the corresponding protection safety stock, and orders the larger of economic lot size and target-minus-inventory-position when a shortage exists. `EOQ = sqrt(2 * annualized_demand * ordering_cost / (unit_cost * holding_rate))`. Quantities round up to case packs and minimum order quantity. Zero-demand EOQ is zero; zero demand makes days of supply undefined. No initial open POs are assumed.

Simulation replays held-out recorded sales as a demand proxy. Opening stock and cost estimates are reconstructed using only data available at test origin. Sequence: opening inventory → receipts → demand/fulfillment/lost sales → inventory review/order. Lead times are rounded normal draws truncated to at least one day; same day-indexed draws compare policies. Inventory position includes open POs. Baseline orders to 14 historical-mean demand days at each review; optimized policy uses the forecast protection window and EOQ. Backorders are not modeled. Closing stock incurs holding cost; fixed cost occurs on placing an order; lost units incur configurable penalty. Procurement spend is not a cost reduction metric because ending stock and unfinished POs have different terminal values.

Fill rate is fulfilled/actual demand. Cycle service counts completed receipt-to-receipt cycles only; first and unfinished cycles are excluded, so short horizons can legitimately yield null. Inventory turnover annualizes fulfilled units/average stock and is a unit proxy. Savings are paired baseline minus optimized cost, averaged across Monte Carlo runs at one service level. Revenue protected is the paired change in lost units valued at price, not incremental realized profit. Short horizons create terminal effects; extend to 90 days, more runs and burn-in before operational use. Sensitivity changes lead time, lead-time variability, holding rate, fixed order cost, forecast error and lost-sale penalty by 0.5×/1.5×, with 90/95/98% service levels in every case.

## Operational risk and root-cause interpretation
Optional classifiers use one order per row and chronological 80/20 dates: logistic regression, shallow tree, random forest and LightGBM. Inputs are market, region, segment, shipping mode and scheduled duration. Actual shipping days, late flags, delivery status, shipping date, realized profit and order status never enter predictors. Report precision, recall, F1, ROC-AUC, PR-AUC and all confusion counts at fixed 0.5. No test-set threshold tuning. Class imbalance uses class weights; calibrate probabilities on separate validation data before treating scores as probabilities. False positives waste expedited capacity; false negatives risk failed promises. Region can proxy socioeconomic conditions; review disparate service outcomes, not individual customer worth. Feature importance and decomposition are associative, not proven bottleneck causes. Carrier scans/capacity and interventions are needed for causal diagnosis.

Raw-file audits expose schema and counts, not string values. Numeric customer identifiers, phone/address/password fields have extrema suppressed. Outliers use the first 10,000 rows with a 1.5-IQR rule and are descriptive flags only. Memory reports peak chunk memory, not whole-process RSS.
